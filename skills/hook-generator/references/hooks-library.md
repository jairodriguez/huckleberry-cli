# Hooks Library

Master collection of attention-grabbing hooks for headlines, opening lines, and content engagement.

## What is a Hook?

A hook is **not** your headline or first line. A hook is something you **add** to your headline or first line to make it interesting. Think of it as the "whipped cream and nuts" you add to a vanilla topic to give it spice and grab attention.

**Process**: Start with your main topic → Add hook elements → Create compelling headline

## Core Hook Types

### 1. **Outcome Hook**
State the specific result your content promises to deliver.

**Formula**: [Action] + [Specific Outcome]
**Examples**:
- "Gain 28 pounds of muscle" (quantified outcome)
- "Build an army of affiliates" (visual imagery when numbers aren't possible)

**Best Practice**: Always use numbers when possible. If you can't quantify, use specific visual imagery.

### 2. **Time Frame Hook** 
Add urgency by specifying when the outcome will happen.

**Formula**: [Outcome] + [Specific Time Frame]
**Examples**:
- "Get your first client in 24 hours"
- "Your back pain gone in seven days"
- "How to do the splits in less than 30 days"

**Best Practice**: Be as specific as possible with timing. "24 hours" beats "quickly."

### 3. **Objection Busting Hook**
Address the main reason people think your promise won't work for them.

**Formula**: [Outcome] + "even if" + [Common Objection]
**Examples**:
- "Get clients from Twitter even if you don't have any followers"
- "How to do the splits in less than 30 days even if you can't touch your toes"

**Best Practice**: Research common objections by reading comments on similar content.

### 4. **Mechanism Hook**
Explain HOW you'll deliver the outcome - the system, process, or method.

**Formula**: [Outcome] + "with" + [Specific System/Process]
**Examples**:
- "Write better headlines with my foolproof five-step system"
- "Burn fat with the all-donut diet"
- "Bulletproof security with our five-star aluminum alloy system"

**Best Practice**: Make the mechanism sound proprietary and intriguing.

### 5. **Contrarian Hook**
Go against common advice or accepted wisdom.

**Formula**: [Opposite of Common Advice] + [Desired Outcome]
**Examples**:
- "Why you should eat carbs to burn fat"
- "The productivity hack that requires doing less work"
- "How being lazy made me more successful"

**Best Practice**: Research what everyone else is saying, then flip it (but only if you can back it up).

### 6. **Negative Hook**
Tell people what NOT to do instead of what TO do.

**Formula**: [What to Avoid] + "if you want" + [Desired Outcome]
**Examples**:
- "Seven foods you must avoid if you want to build muscle"
- "The one headline mistake that's killing your conversions"
- "Stop doing these 5 things if you want to grow on Twitter"

**Best Practice**: Creates curiosity and positions you as the expert who knows the right way.

## Advanced Hook Combinations

You can combine multiple hooks for maximum impact:

**Example**: "How to do the splits in less than 30 days even if you can't touch your toes"
- **Outcome**: "do the splits"
- **Time Frame**: "in less than 30 days" 
- **Objection Buster**: "even if you can't touch your toes"

## Hook Application Framework

### Step 1: Write Your Content First
Create your main content with a placeholder headline.

### Step 2: Identify Your Core Promise
What specific outcome does your content deliver?

### Step 3: Research the Landscape  
What are others saying about this topic? What objections do people have?

### Step 4: Select Your Hook Elements
Choose 1-3 hook types that best fit your content and audience.

### Step 5: Test Multiple Versions
Write several hook combinations and pick the most compelling.

## Hook Quality Tests

**The Curiosity Test**: Does it make you want to know more?
**The Specificity Test**: Is it concrete and tangible?
**The Belief Test**: Can you deliver on the promise?
**The Standout Test**: Is it different from what others are saying?

## Common Hook Mistakes

❌ **Being too vague**: "Get better results" vs "Get 28 pounds of muscle"
❌ **Following the crowd**: Copying what everyone else is doing
❌ **Over-promising**: Claims you can't back up with content
❌ **Under-hooking**: Using vanilla headlines without any hook elements

## Hook Ethics

Hooks are about grabbing attention for genuinely helpful content. It's only manipulative if:
- You can't deliver on your promise
- You're misleading people
- Your content doesn't provide real value

If you have valuable content, you should use hooks to help people discover it.
