# Awareness Level Guide

**Single source of truth for awareness-level-matched copywriting.**
Every piece of marketing copy must identify the reader's awareness level before writing begins.

---

## The 5-Stage Awareness Ladder

| Stage | Definition | What They Know | What They Need |
|-------|-----------|----------------|----------------|
| **1. Unaware** | Doesn't know they have a problem | Nothing about the problem | Problem awareness first |
| **2. Problem-aware** | Knows the problem, doesn't know solutions exist | They have a pain point | Validation + solution discovery |
| **3. Solution-aware** | Knows solutions exist, doesn't know your product | Solutions exist in the category | Differentiation + trust |
| **4. Product-aware** | Knows your product, not convinced it's right | Your product exists | Proof + objection handling |
| **5. Most-aware** | Ready to buy, just needs the offer | Everything about your product | Price, guarantee, urgency |

---

## Workflow: ICP → Awareness → Copy Framework

**MANDATORY ORDER:**
```
1. ICP DEFINITION (who are we talking to?)
   ↓
2. AWARENESS LEVEL (what do they already know?)
   ↓
3. COPY FRAMEWORK SELECTION (how do we approach them?)
   ↓
4. COPY WRITING (execute)
```

**You cannot assess awareness level without knowing the audience.** A developer and a non-technical buyer have different awareness levels for the same product. The ICP informs the awareness assessment.

## Awareness Level Detection

### For SEO Content: Keyword Intent → Awareness Level

| Keyword Pattern | Intent | Awareness Level |
|-----------------|--------|-----------------|
| "how to fix X", "why does X happen", "what is X" | Informational | **Problem-aware** |
| "best X for Y", "X vs Y", "top X" | Comparative | **Solution-aware** |
| "your product name", "brand name", "review" | Navigational | **Product-aware** |
| "buy X", "discount code", "pricing", "order" | Transactional | **Most-aware** |

### For Paid Traffic: Ad Copy + Landing Page Match

| Ad Promise | Landing Page Type | Awareness Level |
|------------|-------------------|-----------------|
| "Solve your [problem]" | Advertorial funnel | **Problem-aware** |
| "Why [competitor] doesn't work" | Comparison page | **Solution-aware** |
| "Try [product] free" | Product page | **Product-aware** |
| "50% off today only" | Direct offer page | **Most-aware** |

### For Social Content: Topic + Audience

| Content Topic | Audience Context | Awareness Level |
|---------------|------------------|-----------------|
| Pain point stories | General audience | **Problem-aware** |
| Category education | Interest-based audience | **Problem/Solution-aware** |
| Product comparisons | Engaged followers | **Solution-aware** |
| Product features | Existing audience | **Product-aware** |
| Limited-time offers | Email subscribers | **Most-aware** |

### For Email: Subscriber Stage

| Email Type | Subscriber Stage | Awareness Level |
|------------|------------------|-----------------|
| Welcome sequence | New subscriber | **Problem-aware** |
| Nurture sequence | Engaged, no purchase | **Solution-aware** |
| Sales sequence | Product page visitors | **Product-aware** |
| Win-back sequence | Lapsed customers | **Most-aware** |

## The 4-Step Advertorial Funnel

**For Problem-Aware and Solution-Aware audiences.**
This is the Google Ads meta that works for ALL traffic sources — paid, organic, social.

### Step 1: Validate the Struggle

**Job:** Make them feel understood. Build rapport through precise pain acknowledgment.

**Copy patterns:**
- "You're not alone..."
- "If you've ever [struggle]..."
- "The reason you [symptom] is..."
- "Most people think [misconception]..."
- "You've tried [common solution] and it didn't work because..."

**Example:**
> "You wake up at 3am again. Staring at the ceiling. You've tried chamomile tea, magnesium, even those sleep podcasts. Nothing sticks. You're not broken — you're just solving the wrong problem."

### Step 2: Explain the Hidden Cause

**Job:** Create cognitive investment. Leaving means leaving with incomplete understanding.

**Copy patterns:**
- "What most people don't realize..."
- "The real cause isn't what you think..."
- "Here's why [common solution] doesn't work..."
- "The problem isn't [surface issue] — it's [root cause]..."
- "Scientists recently discovered..."

**Example:**
> "Here's what nobody tells you: your 3am wake-up isn't a sleep problem. It's a blood sugar problem. When your glucose drops at 2-3am, your body releases cortisol to compensate. That cortisol is what jolts you awake. The chamomile tea? It never had a chance against your biochemistry."

### Step 3: Introduce the Mechanism (Not the Product)

**Job:** Explain HOW the problem gets fixed at a biological or structural level. Trust transfers from author to logic.

**Copy patterns:**
- "Here's how it actually works at the [biological/structural] level..."
- "The solution isn't [surface fix], it's [root cause fix]..."
- "When you [mechanism action], here's what happens..."
- "The science behind this is simple..."

**Example:**
> "The fix isn't about 'relaxing more.' It's about stabilizing your blood sugar overnight. When you give your body a slow-release glucose source before bed, cortisol never spikes. Your brain stays in deep sleep instead of being yanked into light sleep. No jolt. No ceiling staring."

### Step 4: Present the Product as Best Implementation

**Job:** Show your product as the best implementation of a mechanism they already believe in.

**Copy patterns:**
- "The best way to [mechanism] is..."
- "That's why we built [product]..."
- "[Product] is the only [category] that [mechanism]..."
- "Here's how [product] makes this easy..."

**Example:**
> "That's why we built SleepStable. It's the only magnesium supplement with slow-release glucose beads. One capsule before bed stabilizes your blood sugar for 8 hours. No more 3am wake-ups. No more ceiling staring. Just deep, uninterrupted sleep."

## Copy Framework Selection Matrix

| Awareness Level | Recommended Frameworks | Anti-Patterns |
|-----------------|----------------------|---------------|
| **Unaware** | Story-driven, Slippery Slide, Pattern Interrupt | Don't mention product, don't assume problem knowledge |
| **Problem-aware** | PAS, Advertorial Funnel (Steps 1-2), Emotional storytelling | Don't skip to solution, don't pitch product |
| **Solution-aware** | Advertorial Funnel (Steps 2-4), Comparison, Before/After | Don't ignore competitors, don't assume brand trust |
| **Product-aware** | Standard landing page, Social proof, Objection handling | Don't re-explain the problem, don't skip proof |
| **Most-aware** | Direct offer, Urgency, Price anchor | Don't over-explain, don't waste words |

## Architecture Selection by Awareness Level

### For Landing Pages

| Awareness Level | Architecture | Sections |
|-----------------|--------------|----------|
| **Unaware** | Story-first | Story → Problem → Solution → Product → CTA |
| **Problem-aware** | Advertorial funnel | Validate → Cause → Mechanism → Product → CTA |
| **Solution-aware** | Advertorial funnel | Cause → Mechanism → Product → Proof → CTA |
| **Product-aware** | Standard 8-section | Hero → Problem → Value Stack → Proof → Transformation → CTA |
| **Most-aware** | Direct offer | Hero (offer) → Price → Guarantee → CTA |

### For Blog/SEO Content

| Awareness Level | Content Approach |
|-----------------|------------------|
| **Unaware** | Educational, problem-focused, no product mention |
| **Problem-aware** | Problem + solution category, minimal product mention |
| **Solution-aware** | Comparison, "how it works", product as example |
| **Product-aware** | Product review, case study, demo |
| **Most-aware** | Pricing, discount, how to buy |

### For Social Content

| Awareness Level | Content Approach |
|-----------------|------------------|
| **Unaware** | Pattern interrupt, story, curiosity |
| **Problem-aware** | Pain point, relatable moment, "you're not alone" |
| **Solution-aware** | Category education, "did you know" |
| **Product-aware** | Feature highlight, proof, testimonial |
| **Most-aware** | Offer, urgency, CTA |

### For Email

| Awareness Level | Email Approach |
|-----------------|----------------|
| **Unaware** | Welcome, story, problem education |
| **Problem-aware** | Nurture, validation, solution discovery |
| **Solution-aware** | Comparison, differentiation, proof |
| **Product-aware** | Demo, case study, objection handling |
| **Most-aware** | Offer, discount, urgency |

## Anti-Patterns (What NOT to Do)

### DO NOT:
- Send problem-aware traffic to a PDP (product detail page)
- Pitch the product before validating the problem
- Skip the mechanism explanation and jump to "buy now"
- Assume the reader knows your brand
- Assume the reader knows solutions exist
- Use the same copy approach for all awareness levels
- Write product-first copy for informational keywords

### DO:
- Match the copy architecture to the reader's psychological position
- Build rapport before building trust
- Explain the mechanism before presenting the product
- Use the ICP to inform the awareness assessment
- Ask the user if awareness level is unclear from context
- Select the correct framework based on awareness level
- Progress through awareness levels in nurture sequences

## Quick Reference Card

```
UNAWARE      → Story first, problem second, solution third
PROBLEM-AWARE → Validate pain, explain cause, show mechanism
SOLUTION-AWARE → Explain mechanism, differentiate, build trust
PRODUCT-AWARE → Proof, comparison, objection handling
MOST-AWARE   → Offer, price, guarantee, CTA
```

**The #1 conversion killer:** Sending problem/solution-aware traffic to a product page. This skips 3 psychological steps and kills conversion. Always match the page architecture to the reader's awareness level.
